The Tang Dynasty

Three centuries when Chang'an was among the largest cities on Earth, poetry flourished, and the Silk Road carried ideas as well as silk. A guide for students to explore and teachers to use.

Overview

The Tang followed the short-lived Sui dynasty. It united China again, expanded its borders, and welcomed traders, monks and envoys from across Asia. Many historians call it a golden age.

Life and culture

Chang'an, a world city

Laid out on a strict grid with walled wards and two big markets. Merchants from Persia, Central Asia and Korea lived and traded here.

The Silk Road

Caravans carried silk and tea west, and brought horses, glassware, music and new religions east.

Poetry

Li Bai wrote with bold imagination, while Du Fu described the suffering of ordinary people. Over 48,000 Tang poems survive.

Buddhism

The monk Xuanzang travelled to India (629–645) and returned with hundreds of texts. His journey inspired the novel Journey to the West.

Exams and officials

The Tang expanded civil service exams, giving talented men from outside the great families a path into government.

Art and goods

Tang potters made lively tri-color glazed (sancai) horses and camels. Tea drinking spread, and Lu Yu wrote the Classic of Tea.

Legacy

The dynasty gave lasting things to the world. Printing is one: the Diamond Sutra of 868 is the oldest dated printed book. Late Tang alchemists also discovered gunpowder. Even today, many overseas Chinese communities are called "Tang people" and Chinatowns are "Tang streets" in Chinese.

Key words

Chang'an
The Tang capital, modern Xi'an.
Silk Road
A network of trade routes linking China with Central Asia and beyond.
Sancai
"Three colors": glazed pottery in green, amber and cream.
Rebellion
An armed uprising against a ruler or government.
Civil service exam
A test used to choose government officials by ability.

For teachers

Ready-to-use ideas for a lesson or a short unit. Adapt for age and time available.

  1. Chang'an market role play (30 min). Students act as traders, monks and officials, and explain what they carry and why they came.
  2. Poem close reading (20 min). Read a Li Bai or Du Fu poem in translation. What does it show about daily life?
  3. Map the Silk Road (25 min). Mark the route and the goods that moved in each direction.
  4. Two-emperor debate (30 min). Compare Taizong and Xuanzong: who was the better ruler?
  1. Why did the Tang welcome foreigners? What were the risks?
  2. Wu Zetian ruled as emperor from 690 to 705. How do we judge a ruler when sources were written by her critics?
  3. What made a dynasty that peaked around 750 collapse in 907?
  4. Which Tang invention has the most impact on your life today?

By the end, students can:

  • Place the Tang on a timeline and name its main phases.
  • Describe two ways the Tang connected with other cultures.
  • Explain how one event, the An Lushan Rebellion, weakened the dynasty.
  • Use a poem as a primary source.
